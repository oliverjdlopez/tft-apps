# Investigating interactions

Prototype for review; outside runtime discovery.

The aim is to explain what happens when mechanics meet, and whether that
interaction matters in practice. Keep three questions distinct: what the rules
say, what follows from those rules, and what observed play supports.

## Form an explanation worth testing

Identify the claimed interaction and the behavior it is supposed to produce.
“These work well together” is a starting point, not an explanation. Work toward
a concrete account of what one element supplies and how another uses it.

Read the relevant effects and restrictions from available factual sources.
Track conditions such as effect type, trigger, recipient, duration, stacking,
and caps where they matter. Do not fill gaps in wording with remembered rules
from another patch. Identify an unresolved rule explicitly if it determines
whether the interaction exists.

## Trace the mechanism

Follow the proposed chain from trigger to effect. Separate direct statements
from deductions. If several steps depend on unverified behavior, retain them as
hypotheses rather than combining them into a confident conclusion.

Look for the limiting step. A combination may function as described but have
little value because its trigger is infrequent, its benefit arrives too late,
another effect already supplies the benefit, or using it displaces something
more valuable. These are questions to investigate, not assumptions about the
named combination.

Consider the boundary cases that distinguish explanations. If the proposed
mechanism is correct, where should its benefit diminish or disappear? A useful
boundary makes the explanation more specific than “strong things are strong
together.”

## Connect the mechanism to observations

Choose evidence capable of addressing the remaining uncertainty. Rules can
establish eligibility; an explicit source account may describe behavior; holder
or cohort results may describe outcomes. None automatically answers the others.

When using aggregate results, preserve the relevant holder and context. Compare
alternatives that address the hypothesis, and account for differing investment
or correlated board strength where the tools permit. Co-occurrence may reflect
access or convention. A strong combined result does not by itself establish an
extra benefit from the interaction.

If the decisive question requires combat observation or an unavailable rule,
identify that missing evidence. Do not imply a simulation or in-game test was
performed. A supported mechanical deduction can still be useful without an
estimate of its placement value.

## Explain what survives

Present the mechanism, its enabling conditions, and its main limit. Distinguish
verified rules, supported deductions, observed associations, and unresolved
questions in natural prose. Explain the practical implication only as far as
those pieces support it.

Stop when the interaction is explained, disproved, or narrowed to a specific
unknown. Avoid extending an interesting interaction into a build recommendation
unless the evidence also supports the opportunity cost of using it.
