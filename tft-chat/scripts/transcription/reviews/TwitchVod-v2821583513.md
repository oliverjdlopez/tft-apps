## Strategic summary

## Overall strategic read

- Economy should be converted into a defined plan—**Fast 9, controlled roll-down, or tempo leveling**—rather than used to greed indefinitely.
- Board weakness is acceptable only when it is intentional and supported by enough HP, a clear loss-streak payoff, and sufficient time to reach the planned spike. Once HP becomes the limiting resource, stabilize immediately.
- Combat quality comes before cosmetic trait additions: establish a functional frontline, correctly itemize the primary carry, and provide mana support before sacrificing stronger units for another trait.
- Scaling effects are only worthwhile when the board is already strong enough to win the fights needed to cash them in; otherwise, immediate combat power is preferred.
- Contested lines are not automatically unplayable. Evaluate remaining copies, shop access, economy, HP, and whether the augment provides enough immediate power to convert the line.

## Compositions and lines

- **Fast 9:** Survivor can support an economy-heavy Fast 9, while Warpath can support either an open-fort line from a weak opener or a normal tempo game from a strong one. Fast 9 is weaker when important supporting units such as Vex and Shen are underpowered, or when the player is already near elimination.
- **Jax/Kog’Maw reroll:** Particularly strong with **Nova plus one**. Without that setup, leveling is generally preferable to forcing the reroll.
- **Five Anima:** Hitting Fiora during a Stage 4 roll-down can let the board farm Stage 4 and potentially reach five cash-outs. If the board is still weak by 5-1, continuing to value the future cash-out over stabilization is likely no longer justified.
- **Think Fast:** Contesting is a risk rather than an automatic pivot condition. A contested player can still win if the economy and augment generate enough copies and tempo, but low HP and poor hit odds make pivoting more important.
- **Late pivots:** Confirm replacement units, usable components, and completed traits before selling the existing board. Selling the old board too early can remove the frontline, traits, or item holders needed to survive the transition.
- **Timebreaker lines:** Timebreaker is especially effective on Riven and Fizz through their interaction with magic-resistance reduction and ultimate amplification. Riven can still use AD items rather than being forced into a pure magic-damage setup. A Timebreaker Shen version can also substantially improve Nami-based boards.

## Economy, leveling, and tempo

- With **three pairs plus Survivor**, rolling before leveling can be reasonable if the pairs are close to upgrades and immediate stabilization is needed.
- Pre-level first when the next level gives a meaningful board slot, better access to key units, or an important breakpoint. Double-leveling for Urgot is a notable spike.
- Before choosing Fast 9, verify both resources: enough gold to reach and stabilize at 9, and enough HP to survive the delayed strength. A long loss streak at very low HP usually means the plan has changed from greed to immediate stabilization.
- Set an explicit HP limit for greed. Continuing a loss streak after its economic payoff is no longer worth the HP cost is a recurring danger.
- At each major checkpoint—Stage 1 holds, Stage 2 augment, first itemization, 3-2, and 4-2—identify whether the priority is stabilization, direction, economy, or scaling.

## Items and augments

- **Leona:** AP and attack speed are generally preferred Arbiter items; Gold is a reasonable fallback.
- **Milio:** Archangel’s Staff can outperform Jeweled Gauntlet when his kit already supplies critical strike. More generally, built-in critical strike lowers Jeweled Gauntlet’s marginal value.
- Slam useful combat items in Fast 9 games instead of holding components indefinitely; Gunblade is one example of a worthwhile generic slam.
- Put tank items on the primary frontline tank rather than leaving them on temporary holders. Thief’s Gloves can go on a secondary frontline unit such as Blitzcrank.
- A durable board still needs a way to defeat opposing durability: anti-heal and armor/magic shred are important, and damage items alone may fail into healing or resistant frontlines.
- Radiant Silvermere is a legitimate frontline option when the board values durability more than offensive utility; its value depends on the holder and composition rather than being inherently weak.
- Judge augment value through the whole board, not only the augment text or its obvious holder. Hero Augment availability can also matter: if the selected champion is already out of the pool, gold may be given instead.

## Positioning and matchup rules

- Do not default to clumping. Spreading frontline units can be stronger when the opponent can punish grouped units with area damage, targeting, or frontline access.
- Scout each round for corner-dependent enemies and adjust positioning to deny their preferred targets.
- Before adding a trait, check that it does not remove a stronger frontline unit or leave the carry without damage or mana support.

## Open review questions

- At each level breakpoint, was the value of rolling for immediate pairs greater than the value of the next level?
- Were weak boards intentional loss-streak positions, or simply delayed stabilization?
- Did the first completed item preserve HP immediately, or were components held too long for perfect items?
- At 3-2 and 4-2, did the augment fit the board that could realistically be fielded, given HP, economy, lobby tempo, and remaining rounds?
- When a line was contested, were the actual copy count and hit odds sufficient to stay?
- Did the board have both the combat core and the necessary anti-heal/shred before chasing additional traits?
- Were scaling choices made with enough expected winning combats, and were pivots completed before the old board was sold?
