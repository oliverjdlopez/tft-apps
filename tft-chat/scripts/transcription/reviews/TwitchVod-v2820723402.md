## Strategic summary

## Overall strategic read

- Build around the composition’s actual win condition rather than forcing a carry, trait, or template.
- Reroll when the plan depends on several low-cost three-stars, relevant copies are available, and the supporting structure is attainable. Level when the board’s strength comes from expensive units, additional trait slots, or a stronger level-8/9 cap.
- Economy should be converted into either upgrades or tempo. Do not level automatically when an extra unit adds little, but do not slow-roll indefinitely after a three-star is no longer realistic or necessary.
- Health, streaks, and economy can be exchanged differently by composition. Greedy scaling is acceptable only when the board can survive until its payoff; weak or low-HP boards should prioritize stabilization.

## Reroll and leveling lines

- Scout before committing to Akali or Kindred-dependent reroll: count contested copies, assess the odds of completing the line, and compare pivoting to a more flexible Nova board.
- Akali 3 is not mandatory. Continue chasing it with many copies, low contesting, and low-impact level-9 additions; otherwise, Akali 2 plus a stronger level-9 board can be superior.
- Akali’s Nova line is described as strongest with five Nova, Deathblade + Gunblade, and Striker’s Prowess or Giant Slayer as the third item. Its anti-healing and damage-over-time effects need enough time and frontline/control support to finish fights.
- Jax, Urgot, and Nova variants involve a tradeoff between higher Nova value and the flexibility or Kindred access of a Nova-plus-one setup. Do not force the highest theoretical trait count if its units are unavailable.
- Badge is treated as a reroll trait that rewards multiple three-stars, while expensive-unit boards should protect economy for leveling. Avoid combining an expensive-board plan with prolonged low-cost rerolls.
- Aurora/Illaoi reroll wants an Emblem, and playing Aurora while rerolling Illaoi is identified as a particularly strong approach. Akali and Illaoi rerolls should be abandoned or reconsidered if their traits, support units, control, items, or frontline cannot be assembled.

## Augments, traits, and economy

- Worth the Wait can support an Ezreal loss-streak plan, but only if the expected scaling justifies the HP loss and a realistic stabilization point exists.
- Pandora’s Items is stronger when the current board can survive; it may be too slow when immediate combat strength is required.
- Anima +1 substantially improves access to the trait and unit flexibility. Anima should generally farm takedowns rather than cash out immediately when the board can keep winning fights.
- Primal Adapter should remain flexible until components, available carries, and the board’s AD/AP direction become clearer.
- Utility can outperform additional greed or damage: Morgana, crowd control, frontline, or anti-healing may provide more marginal value than another item-generation or offensive unit.
- Timebreaker rerolls and controlled losing can provide future value, but the benefit must be weighed against damage taken. Wisp purchases also have opportunity cost because normally only one can be bought per round; their combat, economy, experience, or high-risk value should fit the immediate plan.
- Riftbeast scaling and Alpha Mark rewards may justify a delayed power spike, but only when the board can reach it.

## Items and removers

- Assign items according to cast patterns and actual unit efficiency, not a preselected holder. Shojin is especially valuable on fast-attacking or frequently casting units; Sona and Ezreal benefit from mana, with Ezreal 4 potentially becoming a major carry.
- Nova Akali’s stated core is Deathblade + Gunblade; Gunblade can provide important full-board healing rather than only self-sustain. Void Staff belongs on a unit whose casts reliably benefit from amplification.
- Reforge when the current carry or board no longer fits the planned item package instead of forcing inefficient combinations.
- Gargoyles can make Jax a viable primary tank when he is consistently targeted. Morgana with Gargoyle’s and Bastion can also form a durable frontline.
- Sunfire’s anti-heal is valuable, but duplicate copies should solve a deliberate burn-coverage problem rather than being built accidentally.
- Use removers proactively when transferring an item creates a meaningful combat upgrade; saving them indefinitely has an opportunity cost.
- Defensive items and frontline resources become more important against AP-heavy opponents. Anti-healing is especially valuable against durable or healing-heavy boards.

## Positioning and combat mechanics

- Hunters benefit from stable target access because their damage increases while staying on the same target; protect them long enough for that amplification to matter.
- Ravagers are strongest against already weakened units, so Inferno burn and anti-heal can help push enemies below their low-health damage threshold.
- Karma’s tether and delayed burst reward access to suitable targets and enemy clustering. Cosmic Hex is often reasonable in the center when opponents clump there, particularly if Victor also benefits.
- Mordekaiser generally wants a central combat entry rather than a corner. Aatrox can move to another position, so evaluate his starting hex based on the desired eventual engagement.
- With substantial health bonuses from hexes, distributing several durable units across the frontline can outperform relying on one main tank: it creates more contact points and reduces the risk of the entire board waiting behind one early death.
- A possible late-game Timebreaker structure uses Aatrox, Shen, Maokai, Ezreal, Cho’Gath, Pantheon, Jhin, and Tahm Kench or another Brawler; Riven and Kai’Sa can replace Cho’Gath when four Timebreakers are required.

## Key decision rules

- Level when the next slot adds a major unit, trait breakpoint, or meaningful combat upgrade; roll when immediate upgrades matter more, the board is bleeding HP, or the composition relies on accessible low-cost three-stars.
- Reassess after each major hit. Stop chasing a carry when contesting, missing copies, or changing board needs make the odds poor.
- Before taking a greedy augment or rerolling, verify the support trait, carry items, frontline/control, breakpoint access, and a realistic fallback.
- Before adding more damage or scaling, ask whether the board instead needs durability, utility, crowd control, or anti-healing.
- Do not treat a composition as complete when its headline carry is found; the surrounding units and trait structure must enable the carry’s win condition.
