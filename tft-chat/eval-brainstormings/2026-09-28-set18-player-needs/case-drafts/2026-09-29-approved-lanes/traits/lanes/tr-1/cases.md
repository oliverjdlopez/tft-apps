## TR-1-C01 — Ahri's Invoker dependence

### Question

Do Ahri endgame boards perform well without Invoker, or are the good results mostly coming from boards where she has an Invoker emblem?

### Sample answer

The available results suggest the Invoker package deserves a separate look, but they do not establish that Ahri needs it. In the inspected September 25 update of [NoxuTFT's Ahri table](https://noxutft.com/tft-champions/ahri), the Invoker Emblem/Jeweled Gauntlet/Deathcap build in “Invoker Ahri” averaged 3.89 over about 4,500 games. The Jeweled Gauntlet/Shojin/Striker's Flail build in “Blossom Ahri” averaged 4.31 over about 1,500. Those are different item-and-board packages; the latter label does not prove the board had no active Invoker.

So there is descriptive support for a successful emblem package, but the requested non-Invoker comparison is unfinished. I cannot yet say how much of Ahri's good performance it accounts for, or whether comparable Ahri boards without Invoker finish worse. The needed distinction is no active Invoker, active Invoker without an emblem on Ahri, and an emblem on Ahri, with the actual breakpoint retained. Ahri's other items, stars and supporting-board quality must remain visible. A lower placement for the emblem group would still be an association, not the benefit of adding an emblem to an otherwise identical game.

Define the family independently of the trait under investigation, using Ahri carry investment and recurring supporting cores. Do not select a family called “Invoker Ahri” and use it as the entire starting population.

Retrieve final units, stars, items and holders, recorded active traits/breakpoints, final level, main tank and secondary-carry investment, placements, and population metadata. Keep no-emblem boards with active Invoker separate from genuinely inactive boards. Compare common cores within overlapping level/star/item strata; match non-emblem completed-item supply and ordinary mana-item patterns, since an emblem consumes a slot. Preserve alternative high-cost support packages instead of presuming only one cap. Report group games and their share of the full Ahri-carry population, placement distribution, top-four/win rates, and uncertainty. Sparse overlap is a finding, not permission to claim dependence.

Actual evidence: N01 supplies two named build rows with rounded counts, ranked labeling and a September 25 update date, but no explicit patch/subpatch, rank, region or observation interval. ## TR-1-C02 — Soraka's fourth Executioner versus Kennen

### Question

Do Soraka boards with four Executioners actually finish better than the three-Executioner versions, or is that mostly because those boards also have Kennen?

### Sample answer

That distinction is still unresolved. The public Soraka results I could inspect do not cross three versus four Executioners with Kennen presence. [NoxuTFT's Soraka table](https://noxutft.com/tft-champions/soraka) has an “Executioner Malphite” carry build averaging 3.84 over 608 games, but the row does not expose the Executioner count or whether Kennen is present. It therefore cannot answer this comparison.

Kennen is a relevant source of confounding: he is a five-cost Executioner, so a stronger four-piece board can also be a board with a better expensive unit. The [trait reference](https://tactics.tools/info/traits) also distinguishes the three- and four-piece tiers; four does not introduce bleed for the first time. Neither fact establishes which Soraka endgame package finishes better.

The useful result would be the placement gap both overall and among comparable Kennen-present boards, together with other observed ways of reaching four Executioners. If the gap remains in those comparisons, it supports a four-piece package association. If the groups have no meaningful overlap, the data cannot separate Kennen from the trait package. I do not yet have the outcome cells needed to choose between those explanations.

That was the direction of the balance changes, but whether it worked remains unresolved. Intent does not establish the outcome.

The inspected [Cassiopeia build table](https://noxutft.com/tft-champions/cassiopeia) includes a Defender carry build at 4.16 over about 1,400 games and a differently itemized “Vanguard Morgana” carry build at 3.71 over about 1,300. Neither label certifies actual Defender count, and the page lacks comparable before-and-after windows. These rows cannot show that 18.3 reduced Cassiopeia's dependence on six Defender.

The missing result is how itemized Cassiopeia's two-, four- and six-Defender variants changed within comparable star, item and frontline groups. A better overall average could merely reflect a different mix of variants. Even a narrowed conditional gap would describe a changed association; simultaneous balance and metagame changes prevent attributing it solely to the trait nerf.
